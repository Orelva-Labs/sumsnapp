"Am I spending too much on food?" is one of the most common money questions. Official statistics give a useful reference point, as long as you know what they measure.

## United States: about $519 a month on food at home

In the US Bureau of Labor Statistics' Consumer Expenditure Survey, the average household spent **$6,224 on food at home in 2024**, up from $6,053 in 2023. That's about **$519 a month**. Households spent another $3,945 on food away from home, bringing total food spending to $10,169 a year, or 12.9% of all household spending.

| | 2024, average US household |
|---|---|
| Food at home (groceries) | $6,224 a year, about $519 a month |
| Food away from home | $3,945 a year |
| All food | $10,169 a year, 12.9% of spending |

"Food at home" is food bought to prepare and eat at home, roughly what you'd spend at the supermarket. It doesn't include household items like cleaning products or toiletries, which many people count as part of their "grocery" bill.

## Germany: about €335 a month on food

According to figures from the Federal Statistical Office (Destatis) reported in 2024, households in Germany spent **around €335 a month on food** on average in 2023. Across the whole economy, food, drinks and tobacco made up about 14% of household consumer spending in 2024.

After the price jumps of 2022 and 2023, food inflation in Germany has calmed down. In January 2026 food prices were 2.1% higher than a year earlier; by August 2026 they were almost flat, up just 0.1% year on year.

## Why your number will be different

An average household is a statistical construct. Your spending depends on:

- **Household size and ages.** Teenagers eat more than toddlers; a single person spends less in total but often more per person.
- **Where you shop.** Prices for the same item can differ noticeably between discounters and full-range supermarkets.
- **What you count as "groceries".** Do toiletries, cleaning products, pet food and drinks count? Statistics usually separate them; your supermarket receipt doesn't.
- **How often you eat out.** Spending more at restaurants and cafés usually means spending less at the supermarket.

So compare yourself with your own past spending first, and with the averages second.

## How to work out your own number

The most accurate way is from your receipts, because they show what you actually bought rather than what you remember.

1. **Collect a month of receipts,** including small top-up shops, bakeries and the pharmacy.
2. **Split them into categories:** groceries, household, personal care, drinks, eating out. Many receipts mix several.
3. **Add up each category** and divide groceries by the number of people in your household to compare.
4. **Repeat next month,** because one month can be unusual (holidays, guests, a big stock-up).
