Search the App Store for "receipt scanner" and you'll find hundreds of apps. Most of them aren't competing with each other, because they solve different problems. The first step is knowing which kind you need.

This comparison is based on the apps' own App Store listings and websites as of October 2026. We make one of these apps, [Sumsnapp](/en/), so we've tried to be fair about where it fits and where it doesn't.

## The four kinds of receipt app

| Kind | What it's for | What it captures | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expense and bookkeeping | Business expenses, reimbursement, tax | Merchant, date, total, tax; sometimes line items | Expensify, Dext, Shoeboxed, SparkReceipt |
| Rewards and cashback | Earning points for scanning receipts | What you bought, for the app's partners | Fetch |
| Budgeting with bank sync | Overall budget across all accounts | Bank transactions (one total per shop) | YNAB, Monarch, Copilot |
| Item-level spending trackers | What you spend on each item and category | Every line on the receipt | Sumsnapp, Grocyy, BiteSpend, Smart Tickets |

### Expense and bookkeeping apps

These are built for freelancers, small businesses and employees submitting expenses. They focus on getting a receipt to an accountant or into bookkeeping software, with tax amounts and categories for accounting. Most store receipts in an online account and integrate with tools like QuickBooks or Xero.

**Choose one if** you need receipts for tax, reimbursement or an accountant. **Less suited to** understanding household spending item by item.

### Rewards and cashback apps

Rewards apps pay you points for scanning receipts, which you can exchange for gift cards. They read what you bought because that purchase data is valuable to their brand partners.

**Choose one if** you want small rewards and are comfortable sharing your purchase data. **Less suited to** tracking your own spending over time, or to keeping your data private.

### Budgeting apps with bank sync

Budgeting apps connect to your bank accounts and categorise transactions automatically. They give the best overview of all your money: rent, subscriptions, transfers, savings. But a bank only sees one total per shop, so a supermarket trip is one line.

**Choose one if** you want a complete budget across accounts. **Less suited to** seeing what you spend on groceries in detail, or to people who don't want to link their bank.

### Item-level spending trackers

A newer group of apps reads every line of a receipt to show what you spend on individual items and categories, often with price comparisons between stores. Several have appeared since 2025, many focused on groceries. They differ in where they read receipts (on the device or in the cloud), whether they need an account, which countries and receipts they support, and price.

**Choose one if** you want to know what you spend on food and everyday items, how prices change and where things are cheaper.

## How to choose

Ask yourself one question: **who is the data for?**

- **Your accountant or employer:** an expense app.
- **A brand, in exchange for rewards:** a rewards app.
- **Your overall budget:** a budgeting app with bank sync.
- **You, to understand what you buy:** an item-level tracker, ideally one that keeps the data on your phone.

Many people use two together, for example a budgeting app for the big picture and an item-level tracker for groceries.
